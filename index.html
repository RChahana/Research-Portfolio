Ongoing Research

Benchmarking Unsupervised and Deep Learning Approaches for UAV Flood Segmentation, with Visual Question Answering for Disaster Assessment

Master's Thesis – Ongoing

Problem Statement

Recent catastrophic events, such as the flooding in Nepal, highlight the need for rapid, accurate situational awareness during natural disasters. While Deep Learning (DL) models achieve high accuracy in flood segmentation, they depend on heavy computation, extensive data labeling, and often generalize poorly to unseen terrain. This research addresses the gap between high-accuracy but resource-intensive DL networks and the lightweight, training-free processing needed on Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) to support immediate relief coordination, by benchmarking unsupervised and deep learning approaches to flood segmentation.

Proposed Methodology

  • Expand the baseline unsupervised approach beyond binary flood segmentation to multi-class segmentation, specifically isolating flooded buildings and roads, addressing a key gap highlighted in the literature.
  • Benchmark against UNet, SegNet, and DeepLabV3+ (EfficientNet/ResNet backbones) in PyTorch, using F1, IoU/Dice, boundary metrics, parameter counts, and inference time.
  • Evaluate generalization and multi-class performance on the independent AIFloodSense multi-class aerial flood dataset.

Expected Outcomes

  • A reproducible benchmark showing when an unsupervised pipeline is sufficient and when deep learning is worth its computational cost.
  • An extended unsupervised pipeline aiming to narrow the accuracy gap with minimal compute.
  • A Visual Question Answering (VQA) layer so responders can ask questions such as “Which roads are flooded?” in natural language.

Timeline

  • ✅ Literature review and baseline reproduction
  • 🔄 Adaptive and multi-class extension
  • ⏳ DL benchmarking
  • ⏳ Cross-dataset validation and thesis defense
  • ⏳ VQA integration
